# Code audit buyer kit

A practical set of Markdown documents for founders, product owners and engineering leads commissioning a scoped code review before launch, a team handover or further development.

Use the kit to agree what will be reviewed, ask for evidence you can evaluate and turn findings into assigned work. It works for internally built, contractor-built and AI-generated software. The questions apply to remote engagements across the US, Canada and Europe without assuming a particular jurisdiction or certification.

## Start here

1. Copy [the acceptance checklist](AUDIT-ACCEPTANCE-CHECKLIST.md) into your project brief. Agree its scope fields with the reviewer before granting access.
2. Use [the finding template](FINDING-TEMPLATE.md) for each observation. Keep observed behaviour, possible impact and unknowns separate.
3. Read [the fictional example](EXAMPLE-FINDING.md) to see how a source-only concern can lead to an investigation without claiming a confirmed incident.
4. At handover, review the coverage record alongside the findings. Give each next action an owner, a decision and an agreed way to verify it.

A checklist item can be **accepted**, **needs clarification** or **outside agreed scope**. Record the reason for the latter two. Completing the checklist means the agreed report has been assessed; it does not mean the application is free of defects.

## What this kit covers

- A clear business decision, review boundaries and evidence requirements.
- Findings with impact, confidence, priority, repair dependencies and verification steps.
- Acceptance of the report and separation of assessment from repairs.

It does not perform a scan, inspect your software or provide a penetration test, incident assessment, legal opinion or compliance certification. Runtime checks require explicit authorisation for the named environment and actions. Review scope and access determine what a reviewer can conclude.
